1The problem

In an open-cast mine, three contractors work one after another. A hold-up at one stage soon stops the stages after it, so the whole line loses output and money.

DrillingContractor A
drilled holes in stock
BlastingContractor B
blasted rock in stock
Loading and haulingContractor C
  • The stage that looks short is often not the one at fault. Loading may stand idle because drilling failed two days earlier.
  • Finding the real cause means reading many shift reports by hand.
  • Contractors dispute the blame, and the loss is hard to put in rupees.

2What the tool does

Records every shift

Holes drilled, explosive used, machines working, rock moved, time lost and the reason, with location, urgency and status.

Separates the blame

For each contractor it splits the shortfall into its own loss and loss from being starved by the stage before.

Names the defaulter

It shows the primary defaulter, the main cause, and the cost in rupees, on one screen.

Shows where and when

A day-by-day grid shows where each delay started. Stock charts show how it spread down the line.

3How it decides who is at fault

  1. Set the plan. From the mine settings: 10,000 BCM, 400 holes per shift, and one blast a day at 15:00. (BCM = cubic metres of rock measured in the ground.)
  2. Compare each stage with its plan, shift by shift.
  3. Check the stock between stages. If the stage before ran out of holes or rock, the loss is "starved" and is not the contractor's fault.
  4. Follow the real cause. If drilling stopped because blasted rock was not cleared, the loss goes to loading and hauling.
  5. Add up each contractor's own loss. The largest is the primary defaulter. Any other stage losing 2% of plan or more is contributing.
  6. Put a price on it. Each BCM not delivered costs ₹150, a rate the team can change.

5How it is built

  • Three web pages written in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, the basic languages of web pages. Nothing to install.
  • Published by Vercel, a free website host, straight from GitHub, where the code is stored.
  • Saves to Supabase, an online database, in one table of shift reports. Until it is switched on, each browser keeps its own reports.
  • Charts by Chart.js, a free charting library.
  • Imports and exports CSV spreadsheet files, ready for scheduled SAP exports.
  • About 2,100 lines of code and drawings, written with Claude, an AI assistant, in the steps on the right.

6Value for MCL

  • Fair blameA contractor is charged only for its own losses, not for being starved by another.
  • Faster answersDefaulter, cause and cost on one screen, instead of reading reports by hand.
  • Early warningStock between stages shows when the next stage is about to stop.
  • Loss in rupeesEvery BCM not delivered is priced, ready for contract discussions.
  • Ready for SAPThe CSV layout can take scheduled exports from SAP.
  • Works anywhereOn a phone at the face, or on a computer in the office.

7Limits and next steps

Limits today

  • All figures are made up for practice. There is no real MCL data.
  • Mine settings are kept in each browser, because the tool uses one table only.

Next steps

  1. Run the database setup and fill in config.js, so the team shares one set of reports.
  2. Decide who is responsible for each delay reason: the contractor, MCL or the weather.
  3. Add a login before any real data goes in, with approval from IT.
  4. Ask IT for scheduled SAP exports that the tool can import.

8How to demo it in five minutes

  1. Open the Dashboard. Read the verdict at the top and the dial showing how much of the plan was delivered.
  2. Walk the production line. Point to the three stage cards and the stock tanks between them.
  3. Tap a box with a border in the day-by-day grid. It shows where a delay started and what it cost.
  4. Show a "starved" day. Loading looked short, but the stage before it was at fault.
  5. Add a shift on the Inputs page with a shovel breakdown, then open the Dashboard again.
  6. Press Print or save as PDF to hand out the report.
